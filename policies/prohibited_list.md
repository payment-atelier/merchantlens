# Prohibited and Restricted Categories

Synthetic stand-in policy for the MerchantLens prototype. Not real platform policy.

Two tiers. **Prohibited** categories may not be sold on the platform at all. **Elevated-risk**
categories are permitted but require risk terms — they are not violations and must never be
screened as such.

---

## Section 1 — Prohibited categories

A catalog containing any item in this section is a policy violation. Cite the specific listing.

**1.1 Vape, e-cigarette and nicotine delivery products.**
Disposable vapes, refillable vape devices and pod kits, e-liquid and nicotine salts, heated
tobacco units, nicotine pouches, and replacement coils or cartridges sold for these devices.

**1.2 Counterfeit and replica branded goods.**
Items presented as, or reasonably identifiable as, unauthorised copies of a branded product.
Signals include "inspired by", "dupe", "replica", "in the style of" or "AAA" alongside a brand
name; brand names used in titles by a seller with no stated authorisation; and price points
substantially below the market rate for the named brand.

**1.3 Cannabis, CBD and hemp-derived consumables.**
Cannabis flower, edibles, vapes and concentrates, and ingestible or topical CBD products,
including hemp-derived cannabinoids sold for consumption.

**1.4 Firearms, ammunition and regulated weapon parts.**
Firearms and frames, ammunition and reloading components, suppressors, and high-capacity
magazines.

**1.5 Adult content and services.**
Pornographic media, adult live services, and sexual services of any kind.

**1.6 Gambling and games of chance for real value.**
Casino play, sports betting, lotteries, and loot mechanics redeemable for real-world value.

**1.7 Prescription pharmaceuticals.**
Any medicine requiring a prescription, and any product marketed as a substitute for one.

**1.8 Live animals.**
Live animals of any species, including insects and aquatic livestock.

**1.9 Recalled or unsafe goods.**
Items subject to an active safety recall, and goods that fail applicable safety standards for
their category.

---

## Section 2 — Elevated-risk categories

Permitted. These are **not** violations. Presence in a catalog supports a risk-terms
recommendation, never a decline and never a policy violation verdict.

**2.1 Dietary supplements, vitamins and nutraceuticals.**
Protein powders, multivitamins, creatine, fish oil, electrolyte and greens powders, and similar
ingestible supplements. Elevated for claim-substantiation and chargeback exposure only.

**2.2 Made-to-order and pre-order goods with extended fulfilment.**
Any catalog whose stated lead time places delivery materially after payment. Elevated for
dispute exposure across the payment-to-fulfilment gap.

**2.3 Subscription and recurring-billing offerings.**
Elevated for cancellation and recurring-charge dispute exposure.

**2.4 High-ticket single items.**
Individual items priced well above the category norm. Elevated for dispute value concentration.

---

## Section 3 — How to apply this list

1. Screen the **catalog as sold**, not the stated business type. A merchant may state a
   permitted category and list prohibited items; the catalog governs.
2. A Section 1 hit is a **violation**. Name the specific listing titles that triggered it.
3. A Section 2 hit is **not** a violation. Record it as an elevated-risk factor. Sections 2.1,
   2.2 and 2.4 describe goods the merchant sells, and those support a risk-terms
   recommendation. Section 2.3 describes how a merchant bills rather than what they sell — note
   it, but a subscription option on its own does not move an otherwise clean merchant off
   "clear".
4. If an item is ambiguous between sections, do not guess. Flag it for analyst judgment and
   state both readings.
