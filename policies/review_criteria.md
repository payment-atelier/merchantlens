# Merchant Review Criteria

Synthetic stand-in policy for the MerchantLens prototype. Not real platform policy.

Four sections. Cite the section number when a criterion drives a verdict.

---

## Section 1 — Account and compliance information

**1.1 Identity and ownership.** The named owner and ownership percentage must be present and
internally consistent with the application.

**1.2 Registration consistency.** The bank account name should match the registered legal name.
A mismatch is not automatically adverse — it may reflect a holding structure — but it is
unexplained until documented, and an unexplained mismatch requires documentation before a
decision.

**1.3 Tax status.** A pending tax identification status is a documentation gap, not a decline
reason.

---

## Section 2 — Business practices and customer experience

**2.1 Accuracy of product descriptions.** Listings must describe what is actually sold. Vague,
placeholder or absent descriptions prevent assessment and route to documentation.

**2.2 Counterfeit and misrepresented goods.** Goods presented as branded items without evident
authorisation, or described so as to imply a brand association that is not claimed outright,
are a business-practices failure. See `prohibited_list.md` Section 1.2.

**2.3 Fulfilment promises against the business model.** Stated shipping and lead times must be
plausible for the goods sold. Extended lead times are permitted and are an elevated-risk factor,
not a failure, provided they are disclosed on the storefront.

**2.4 Refund and cancellation terms.** A published refund policy with specific windows and
conditions is expected. Unedited template text counts as absent.

---

## Section 3 — Business credibility

**3.1 Storefront substance.** A storefront must carry enough genuine content to determine what
the business sells. Listing count alone does not establish this — description substance, page
authenticity and policy specificity do.

**3.2 Authenticity of claims.** Credentials, certifications, awards and origin claims stated on
the storefront should be specific enough to be checkable. Unsupported superlatives are a weak
signal on their own.

**3.3 Category coherence.** A catalog that spans several unrelated categories, or whose
composition contradicts the stated business type, requires explanation.

---

## Section 4 — Transaction and operational patterns

**4.1 Volume plausibility against catalog scale.** Projected monthly volume should be plausible
for the size and price points of the catalog. A material gap requires documentation.

**4.2 Processing history.** Absence of processing history is not adverse in itself, but it
removes a corroborating signal and raises the weight of the remaining evidence.

**4.3 Rapid volume increases.** A projected volume materially above the merchant's own trailing
pattern requires explanation.

**4.4 High-stakes volume.** Projected monthly volume at or above $250,000 with no processing
history requires human review regardless of how clean the remainder of the file is.
